# Evaluating Sources

Choose evidence for the claim, not for the topic in the abstract.

## Evaluation dimensions

Consider the dimensions that can change the conclusion:

- **Proximity:** How directly does the source observe, define, or produce the fact?
- **Authority:** Is the author or publisher responsible for, qualified to study, or
  demonstrably expert in the claim?
- **Method:** Are the data, derivation, test conditions, definitions, and assumptions
  inspectable?
- **Currency:** Does the claim depend on a date, release, edition, jurisdiction, or
  other changing context?
- **Independence:** Do corroborating sources rely on genuinely separate evidence?
- **Reproducibility:** Can the observation, calculation, query, or code path be checked?
- **Incentives and limitations:** What might the source omit, simplify, or frame
  selectively?

No fixed ranking works for every question. Original research, official records,
standards, first-party documentation, authoritative datasets, source code, tests, and
direct observations are often primary evidence. Strong secondary sources are valuable
for interpretation and synthesis. Do not reject tertiary, community, or obscure sources
solely because of their category or visibility. A forum post may contain firsthand
observations, reproducible tests, or a useful explanation. Assess its support for the
particular claim and distinguish corroborated information, reproducible observations,
and unverified assertions. State concrete material concerns where the source is used;
for example, an anonymous report includes test code but has not been independently
reproduced. Do not silently use weak evidence as the sole basis for a material conclusion
or substitute generic trust scores for an assessment.

## Corroboration

Corroborate selectively. Seek independent support when a claim is central, disputed,
counterintuitive, highly precise, based on a source with material limitations, or likely
to cause harm or wasted work if wrong. Do not require multiple sources for routine facts
already established by a definitive primary source.

When sources conflict:

1. Verify that they address the same question.
2. Compare definitions, scope, population, assumptions, methods, and dates.
3. Check whether one source is describing a contract while another reports an
   observation or exception.
4. Prefer stronger evidence, but preserve a real unresolved disagreement.
5. State what additional evidence would resolve the issue when that matters.

## Current and changeable information

For changeable claims, capture the relevant effective date, release, revision, or access
date. Prefer current first-party material, but consult historical versions when the
question is historical. Do not blend evidence from different contexts as though it
described one stable state.

## Excerpts and citations

Choose excerpt length for its purpose. An annotated source report needs complete
explanatory units, often a paragraph or connected paragraphs where permitted, rather
than isolated supporting sentences. In a synthesized report, select excerpts that clarify
wording or make important reasoning inspectable. Avoid padding and quotation quotas.

Identify each excerpt's source nearby: title, author or responsible organization when
known, source type, direct link, and useful page, section, or other locator. Include a
date or version when it affects interpretation; use shortened labels for repeat sources.
Do not invent missing attribution. A citation must support its attached claim; a
bibliography alone does not make unsupported prose trustworthy.

Verify quotations against the inspected source, including equations and qualifiers.
Check surrounding context for conditions or caveats that affect the meaning. Mark
omissions and editorial insertions; keep separate passages visibly separate. Never
present reconstructed wording, paraphrase, or uncertain OCR as a verified quotation.
Exact quotation establishes what the source says, not that the claim is true.

Keep source text visibly separate from agent explanation and interpretation. Added
factual background needs its own evidence; do not imply it comes from the quoted passage.
Explain material source limitations at the point of use.

Respect applicable copyright and tool quotation limits. If they prevent the requested
report format, disclose the limitation and provide permitted excerpts and precise
passage links or locators. Do not silently substitute an agent-written essay or claim
that the requested excerpt coverage was achieved.
