# Evidence Pack Contract

Use an evidence pack as working material for a substantial investigation or as a handoff
to another writing workflow. Keep it proportional: bullets are enough for a small set of
claims; use a table only when repeated fields make comparison easier.

## Scope

Record:

- the research question and intended use;
- the audience and required depth;
- relevant context boundaries;
- what is explicitly out of scope.

## Claim ledger

For each important claim, capture the fields that affect trust:

- **Claim:** A precise statement that can be evaluated.
- **Status:** Confirmed fact, inference, hypothesis, recommendation, or unknown.
- **Evidence:** Direct link or local reference with a useful locator.
- **Source basis:** Primary evidence, secondary interpretation, or direct observation.
- **Confidence:** High, medium, or low, with a short reason when not obvious.
- **Context:** Date, edition, implementation, jurisdiction, environment, release, or
  other boundary when relevant.
- **Caveat or conflict:** Limitations, contradictory evidence, or competing
  interpretations.
- **Use:** Why the claim matters to the answer or document.

Confidence describes the support for the stated claim within its context. It is not a
substitute for evidence and must not be silently raised during later writing.

## Source excerpts

Save only excerpts likely to establish exact wording, definitions, constraints, or
disagreements. Include the source, locator, supported claim, and a note explaining the
connection. Preserve enough surrounding context to avoid changing the meaning.

## Local and code evidence

When local artifacts matter, record paths, symbols or headings, relevant revision when
needed, and whether behavior was read, executed, tested, or inferred. A code path can
establish what an implementation does; it does not automatically establish what every
implementation should do.

## Gaps and visual opportunities

List unresolved questions and evidence that would reduce the uncertainty. Also record
relationships, sequences, comparisons, spatial ideas, or quantitative data that may be
clearer as a diagram, table, chart, or sourced image. These are candidates, not a quota.

## Handoff rule

A later author may simplify presentation but must preserve the claim's scope, confidence,
and caveats. If the planned document needs a stronger claim than the evidence supports,
return to research rather than smoothing over the gap.
